# Task Scenarios — Lapse

Read the **Say this** column aloud, word for word. It is phrased as a goal, never
as an instruction: naming a control tells the participant where to look and
destroys the finding you were trying to collect.

Start the app on the Stopwatch tab, with History empty and settings at their
defaults, before every session.

---

### T0 — First impressions *(not scored)*

> **Say this:** "Before you touch anything — what do you think this app does?
> Now take a minute to look around and tell me what you notice."

Record their words verbatim. This is the only chance to capture an untainted
first reading of the interface.

---

### T1 — Time a run with laps

> **Say this:** "You're timing someone running four laps of a track. Time the
> whole run, and record each lap as they pass you."

**Expected outcome:** Starts the stopwatch, presses Lap four times, lap rows appear with split and total.

**Watch for:** Do they find Lap without prompting? Do they understand the two
number columns — do they read the split, the total, or misread one as the
other? Any comment on the running total?

---

### T2 — Interpret the lap data

> **Say this:** "Which of those laps was the fastest?"

**Expected outcome:** Reads it off the FASTEST tag, or compares the split column.

**Watch for:** Do they notice the tag at all, or scan the numbers manually? Do
they look in the correct (split) column? Does anyone try to sort or tap a row?

---

### T3 — Finish and keep the run

> **Say this:** "The run's over. Make sure it's saved so you can look at it
> later, and get the stopwatch ready for a completely new run."

**Expected outcome:** Stops, then presses Reset — which files the run into History and clears the display.

**Watch for:** This is the deliberately non-obvious one. Does the word "Reset"
read as *discard* rather than *save*? Do they hesitate, look for a separate
save control, or ask whether it will be lost? After pressing it, do they
believe the run was kept? Record hesitation even when the task succeeds.

---

### T4 — Retrieve a past run

> **Say this:** "Find that run again and tell me how long the third lap took."

**Expected outcome:** History tab → taps the run → reads lap 3's split on the detail screen.

**Watch for:** Do they go to History unprompted? Is it obvious a row can be
opened? Do they read the right row and column?

---

### T5 — Set a duration with no button

> **Say this:** "Set a timer for exactly seven minutes and start it."

**Expected outcome:** Selects a preset then uses **+1 min** to reach 7:00, and starts.

**Watch for:** There is no 7m preset, so this forces discovery. Do they find the
±1 min controls? Do they first try to tap the big time display to edit it
directly? Do they try to hold a preset? How long before they succeed, and do
they express frustration at not being able to type a value?

---

### T6 — Reuse a custom duration

> **Say this:** "Stop that. Now set up another seven-minute timer."

**Expected outcome:** Notices the **7m** chip that has appeared under RECENT and taps it.

**Watch for:** Run this *after* T5, since the chip only exists once a custom
duration has been started. Do they spot the new RECENT row, or repeat the
nudging from scratch? If they miss it, do they notice when it is pointed out
afterwards, and what do they say about it?

---

### T7 — Adjust the app to a situation

> **Say this:** "You're going to use this in a dark room, and you find the
> fast-changing decimal numbers distracting. Set the app up to suit that."

**Expected outcome:** Settings → Theme → Dark, and Show hundredths → off.

**Watch for:** Do they find Settings? Do they complete both halves, or stop
after one? Is "Show hundredths" understood as the decimals they were shown?

---

### T8 — Persistence expectations

> **Say this:** "Start timing something. Now close the app completely — as if
> you were done with your phone. Before you reopen it: what do you expect to
> see?"

**Expected outcome:** Predicts something, then reopens to find the stopwatch still running and correct.

**Ask before reopening**, and record the prediction verbatim. Then reopen and
record their reaction.

**Watch for:** Do they expect it to have reset, paused, or kept running? Is the
actual behaviour a pleasant surprise, or unwanted? This task speaks directly to
the "were user expectations met" part of the brief, so capture their exact words.

---

### T9 — Open exploration *(optional, if time allows)*

> **Say this:** "Is there anything you expected to find that isn't here?"

Free exploration. Note any feature they hunt for and cannot find.
